Key question · To put different neural networks on the same scale, what rules are needed?

We put the atlas's 42 neural networks into a robot brain the size of a fruit fly, one at a time. First, the rules are made public: how the five stats on the spec sheet were set, how the battle math works, and how power classes and bosses were decided. You can also change the rules yourself and see how the conclusions shift.

1Spec sheet

How to read the spec sheet

The five stats on the robot spec sheet come in two kinds. ATK and HIT are judged values: relative ratings from 1 to 5 set by people under the same assumed conditions. ATK matches the 'expressiveness' bar in the atlas, and HIT is set separately to measure how accurately a trained model hits new inputs (real-world accuracy). GAN is hard to train, but once trained its results are stable, so this value differs from the atlas's 'training stability'. SPD, EN, and MEM are rule-based values: matched to the rule table in the next section by asking 'how many calculation steps for one new input?'

ATK Attack power

Expressiveness

The capacity to hold complex patterns. Sets the upper limit of accuracy.
HIT Hit rate

Real-world accuracy

How accurately a trained model hits new inputs it has not seen. Hit chance = 0.5 + 0.1 × HIT
SPD Mobility

Response speed

Number of calculation steps to answer one new input. At 2 or lower, it is one turn late on reflex tasks.
EN Efficiency

Energy saving

EN consumed per attack: 5→2, 4→5, 3→10, 2→30, 1→100
MEM Memory

Memory over time

Damage in sequence, memory, and algorithm tasks × (1 + 0.1 × (MEM − 3))

2Rule table

How the rule-based values were set

The three rule-based values were assigned by how the model computes, not by model size. For example, a diffusion model "repeats denoising for hundreds to 1,000 steps," so it gets SPD 1, and a Transformer "re-references the entire past context for every new input," so it gets EN 1. The supporting sentences for all 42 can be viewed for each model in the page 4 hangar.

Limits of this rule · Even with the same structure, actual speed and power can differ several-fold depending on size, quantization, and hardware. Rule values are grades that capture only the 'difference in calculation method'.

3Formula

Step-by-step battle calculation

Formula (the same formula is used for every battle)

Damage per hit = 10 × ATK × 1.2 (specialty boss bonus) × memory adjustment · number of attacks = min(10, ⌊battery ÷ EN per attack⌋) − slow response 1 · total damage = number of attacks × damage per hit × hit chance · boss HP 200

4Power class and bosses

Four power classes and nine bosses

Even the same model can fire a different number of times depending on the power source. Each model fights its own specialty boss, and to compare models fairly, they also all fight the common boss 'All-Round Exam Chimera'. The common boss has no bonus, memory adjustment, or response penalty, and rankings are based only on common-boss results.
