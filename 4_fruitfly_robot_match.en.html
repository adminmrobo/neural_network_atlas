2Power class

Power ladder · Who gets stronger when the power source goes up?

This ladder is not a performance ranking

Common bosses are set up with the same calculation for models that do different jobs, such as classification, generation, and memory. Don't read it as 'DFF is best for small robots'. Use the boss buttons below to compare models that do the same job. The connectome model is left out of the ranking.

Discussion · Three reasons not to take this ranking at face value

  1. ATK and HIT are values people judged. Changing just one cell in the page 3 lab moves the ranking.
  2. SPD·EN·MEM are only grades of calculation method. Even the same structure can differ several-fold in real speed and power depending on size, quantization, and hardware.
  3. Real performance depends largely on the amount and quality of data and the training method. This battle assumes all of these are the same.

In field termsFor devices with fixed power and response time, such as drones, small sensors, and robot arms, 'the model that survives on budget to the end' comes before 'the most accurate model'. Put latency and power budgets into the test items before deployment.

Limit of the analogy ①

Robot battles are a device to make performance comparisons easy to remember. ATK·HIT are judged values, and SPD·EN·MEM are also simplified grades, so real accuracy and speed vary greatly with data, model size, and hardware.

Limit of the analogy ②

Deep learning did not imitate the brain directly; it borrowed its framework from the brain. The fruit fly brain is believed not to learn through backpropagation.

Limit of the analogy ③

A connectome is only structure. Synaptic strengths and neuron properties must be estimated separately, so a wiring-diagram model does not fully reproduce the fruit fly brain.

In generative AI terms, large language models like ChatGPT stack DFF from box ① and residual connections from box ② on top of the Transformer in box ④ (some use MoE). For image generation, the diffusion model in box ⑤ uses a U-Net or a Transformer as its noise predictor.

Limit: real services have dozens to hundreds of layers and tens of billions of values or more, and data and training procedures strongly determine performance.

Seven items to check before field deployment Example

  1. Latency budget Time allowed from input to answer (e.g., 100 ms per inspection line)
  2. Power and heat budget Range the battery, power supply, and cooling can handle
  3. Memory Whether the model size and intermediate calculations fit on the device
  4. Data and labels Whether the amount is sufficient, includes defective examples, and whether labels are consistent
  5. Validation split Whether training, validation, and test sets are split in time order
  6. Monitoring and retraining Detecting when field data changes and deciding when to retrain
  7. Fallback rule A route to hand off to a person or a rule when the model is not confident
