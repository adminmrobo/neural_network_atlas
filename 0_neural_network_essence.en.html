This page is an essence that carries over the first explainer, which started the series, almost as it was. It skims 27 structures on one sheet; the details continue in the atlas on pages 1 and 2, and the performance-comparison game continues in the robot battle on pages 3 and 4.

Something to know first: this chart draws only the "wiring diagram"

The original chart shows only the types of cells (nodes) and the connecting lines. The activation function (how the signal gets bent), the loss function (what counts as a mistake), the training method (how the weights get corrected), and the amount of data are all left out, as if they were fixed.

So the ratings on this page follow the same assumption. We compare "all else being equal, what is the connection structure alone good for?"

However, a few networks are set apart not by their structure but by exactly those missing elements. For example, RBF looks identical to FF in the diagram and differs only in the activation function. For such cases, the card marks it separately as a difference not shown in the diagram.

Cell legend

  • Expressiveness How well it can capture complex patterns (accuracy ceiling)
  • Training stability Does it converge well even when a beginner runs it?
  • Current use How widely it is used in industry and research in 2026

The stars are relative ratings that assume the same conditions. Actual accuracy varies greatly with the data and the problem.

Pick at a glance

What I want to doStructure to look at firstReason
Classifying and predicting tabular numeric dataDFF (MLP), SVMThe structure is simple and stable. With little data, SVM is strong too.
Image recognitionDCN + DRN (residual connections)Convolution builds the prior knowledge that "nearby pixels are looked at together" into the structure.
Time series, sensors, sentencesLSTM, GRUMemory cells carry past information for a long time. These days the Transformer is also widely used.
Generating new dataVAE, GANVAE is stable; GAN is sharp but tricky to train.
Noise removal, anomaly detectionDAE, AEIt learns to reconstruct normal data, and if reconstruction fails, it treats the input as an anomaly.
Very deep modelsDRNThanks to shortcut connections, it trains even beyond 100 layers.
